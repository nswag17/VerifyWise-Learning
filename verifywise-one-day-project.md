# One-day project: build an AI policy starter kit

## Goal

Help a fictional organization decide how it should use AI responsibly, then model that decision inside VerifyWise.

This is a good first project because it teaches both:

- **AI policy thinking** — what is allowed, what needs review, and what needs evidence
- **coding thinking** — how a real app stores information and moves it between the frontend and backend

## Project idea

Imagine a small nonprofit, school, or public-interest team that wants to use an AI chatbot to answer common questions.

Your job is to help them answer:

- What is this AI system for?
- What should it never do?
- What risks does it create?
- What evidence should we keep?
- Who is allowed to approve it?

## What she should create

By the end of the day, she should have:

- a short AI policy draft
- one AI use case
- three risks
- three controls or safeguards
- one evidence note or supporting document
- one short reflection on what she would change in a real organization

## Why this is a good beginner project

It is small enough to finish in a day, but it still teaches real concepts:

- AI is not magic; it needs rules
- policy is easier to understand when it is attached to a concrete use case
- risks and controls come in pairs
- software is usually just a structured way to store and review decisions

## Step 1: pick a fictional organization

Use a simple story, such as:

- a university student support office
- a city library
- a local nonprofit
- a small HR team
- a policy lab

Keep it simple. The point is to learn, not to invent a perfect scenario.

## Step 2: pick one AI use case

Choose one example that is easy to understand.

Good examples:

- a chatbot that answers FAQ questions
- a tool that summarizes meeting notes
- a system that drafts policy text
- a classifier that routes support requests

Bad first examples:

- a fully autonomous hiring system
- a model that makes legal decisions by itself
- a huge multi-department AI program

## Step 3: write the policy in plain English

Ask a free AI tool to help draft plain-language policy text.

Useful prompts:

- “Rewrite this policy so a non-technical person can understand it.”
- “Turn these bullet points into a short internal policy.”
- “What are the risks in this AI use case?”
- “What controls would reduce those risks?”

A good policy draft should say things like:

- what the system may be used for
- what humans must review
- what data should not be entered
- when escalation is required
- how often the use case should be reviewed

## Step 4: add risks and controls

Pick three common AI risks:

- hallucinations or incorrect answers
- privacy or sensitive-data leakage
- bias or unfair treatment

Then pair each risk with a control:

- human review before release
- limit the kinds of data entered into the system
- keep an evidence trail
- test the system before using it
- restrict who can approve changes

## Step 5: add evidence

Evidence can be very simple.

Examples:

- a meeting note
- a policy draft
- a screenshot
- a checklist
- a review note from a manager

The point is to understand that governance is not only about rules — it is also about proof.

## Step 6: use VerifyWise to model the project

Inside VerifyWise, try to map your fictional scenario into the app’s main ideas:

- the organization
- the AI use case
- the policy
- the risks
- the evidence
- any framework or compliance references that matter

If you do not know where something lives, that is okay. Part of the exercise is learning the app structure.

## Step 7: explain the result in one paragraph

At the end, write a short summary like this:

> We created a policy for a chatbot that answers support questions. The biggest risks were incorrect answers, privacy leakage, and bias. We added human review, data limits, and evidence tracking to reduce those risks.

If she can explain the project in her own words, she learned the important part.

## Optional coding stretch goal

If she wants a tiny coding challenge, change one visible label in the frontend.

For example:

- find a heading or button label
- change the text
- refresh the app and see the update

This is a very small change, but it teaches the basic coding loop:

1. find a file
2. change it
3. save it
4. reload the app
5. see the result

That loop is the foundation of coding.

## Done when

The project is finished when she can answer these questions:

- What is the AI system for?
- What could go wrong?
- What would you do about it?
- What evidence would you keep?
- Where would you look in the app if you wanted to edit the policy later?

## Final takeaway

This project is not about building the most impressive AI system.
It is about learning how policy, risk, and software fit together.
